How Hexapus works

Hexapus is a fair-launch meme launchpad and AMM on Arc, Circle's chain where USDC is the native gas token. Every coin starts on a bonding curve, and graduates into a Uniswap v3 pool whose liquidity is locked permanently.

Two things follow from Arc that no other chain gives you:

  • Buying is one transaction. USDC is the native asset, so a purchase is a plain payable call — no approve step, no wrapped token, no second asset to hold for gas.
  • Prices are in dollars and stay there. Every coin opens at a $2,000 market cap and it is still $2,000 tomorrow. Launchpads quoted in SOL or ETH restate every coin's value whenever the quote asset moves, even when nobody trades.

Launching a coin

Launching takes two transactions and about twelve blocks between them.

There is nothing to configure. Every coin uses the same curve, so every launch starts on exactly the same terms — 79.31% of supply sells on the curve, and the remaining 20.69% seeds the pool at the price the curve closed at.

Anti-snipe protection

Arc has a public mempool and first-come-first-served ordering, with no MEV protection. A bot can see transactions and race them. Hexapus does not pretend otherwise — it attacks the two halves of the problem separately.

Before the launch: commit–reveal

Between your commit and your reveal, the name, symbol, image, curve parameters and even the contract address are all unknown, because the address derives from a secret salt. A bot cannot pre-position against a coin it cannot identify.

What this does not do: once the reveal is in the mempool, everything is public. A bot that indiscriminately buys every reveal still gets in early. Commit–reveal does not stop a bot from being fast — it stops speed from being worth anything in advance.

After the launch: a decaying sell tax

Selling in the opening window costs almost everything, decaying linearly to 1% over the first minutes. Buying early is never blocked — blocking early buyers just moves the game to who gets allowlisted. Instead the opening is a terrible time to flip and a fine time to hold.

The tax is not revenue. It stays in the curve, so it ends up in the liquidity pool at graduation. A snipe becomes a subsidy to everyone still holding.

Per-wallet cap

During the opening window each wallet can buy at most 0.5% of supply, which at the opening price is about $10. That is deliberately small: whoever gets in first should not be able to take the cheapest tokens in bulk. A cap alone is defeated by splitting across wallets, and no chain gives us sybil resistance; it bounds one actor's blast radius rather than stopping them. The tax is the part that works, because splitting wallets does not reduce it.

Trading & fees

Launching a coin costs 0.5 USDC, charged when the coin is actually created — not when you commit. An abandoned commit produces no coin and costs nothing but its own gas.

The trading fee is 1% of the USDC leg, on both buys and sells.

Those are shares of the 1% fee, not of the trade. On a $1,000 buy the fee is $10, split $4 / $4 / $2. Fees accrue and are claimed on demand — nothing is ever pushed to you, because a push-based split lets one hostile or blocked recipient break trading for everybody on that coin.

Creator & referral fees

Creators earn 40–50% of every trading fee on their coin, for as long as it trades on the curve, and half of the pool's fees after it graduates.

Creators may buy their own launch with no cap, and it is publicly recorded. Every coin page shows "creator holds N%" whether the creator likes it or not.

A cap was tried and dropped, because it protects less than it looks like it does: nothing stops a creator buying from a second wallet, where the same purchase is invisible. What actually constrains the behaviour is the sell tax, which applies to the creator exactly as it does to anyone else — a large opening buy dumped in the first minutes gives up most of itself to the curve, and that tax stays with the remaining holders. Disclosure plus a cost to dumping beats a limit that a second address defeats.

Referrals

Share a coin's referral link and you earn 20% of the trading fee from everyone who arrives through it — permanently, from their first trade onward, without them needing to use the link again. Attribution is single-tier: if someone you referred refers a third person, that third person's fees go to them, not to you.

Referrals apply to the launch phase. After graduation, fees come from the pool's own fee tier and accrue in aggregate — there is no per-trade record to attribute, so the split becomes 50/50 protocol and creator. Any launchpad promising lifetime referral revenue on an open AMM pool is either routing everything through a bypassable router, or being imprecise.

Graduation

When the curve sells out, anyone can call migrate(). In one atomic transaction it creates a Uniswap v3 pool at the 1% fee tier, seeds it with the raised USDC and the reserved supply, and mints the position to the locker.

The pool opens at the same price the curve closed at — the supply split is chosen so the two prices agree to within 0.007%, leaving no gap for anyone watching the migration to arbitrage.

What "liquidity locked" means here

The locker contract has no withdraw function. Not a timelocked one, not a multisig-gated one — none:

no withdraw    no unlock       no emergency exit
no owner       no admin        no upgrade path
no timelock    no governance   no migration hatch

The only function that touches the pool is collectFees(), and it calls Uniswap's burn with a hardcoded zero, which updates fee accounting without removing liquidity. There is no variable anywhere in that file capable of holding a burn amount. Nobody can pull the liquidity, including us.

Swap

Swap lets you spend USDC from any chain on any Arc token, using Arc's Unified Balance — your USDC across every supported chain treated as one balance, delivered through Circle's App Kit on top of Circle Gateway.

Coins launched elsewhere are routable too, with an additional protocol fee. No creator or referrer share applies to those, since Hexapus did not launch them.

Swap

Moving USDC to Arc uses CCTP v2 natively — burned on the source chain, minted on Arc, no wrapped assets and no third-party bridge risk.

In practice you rarely need it as a separate step: the buy flow batches the Gateway mint and the purchase under a single signature, so bringing money from Base and buying a coin on Arc is one confirmation.
